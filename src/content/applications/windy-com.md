Windy is the map most pilots already have open on the kitchen table. It shows the wind as a moving field, then lets you switch the same map to temperature, pressure, humidity, cloud base, cloud, and rain. Ivo Lukačovič launched it in November 2014 in the Czech Republic. The wind animation comes from Cameron Beccario's open-source earth project.

The point for free flight is not the animation itself. It is being able to change height and model until the day makes sense.

## What to look at

- Wind at the surface and at flying heights. A sea breeze or a valley wind that is fine at takeoff can be a different direction a few hundred meters up.
- A forecast sounding, so you can see inversions, the cloud base, and whether the wind increases as you climb.
- Rain and cloud cover, which tell you if the thermals will be usable or if a cu-nim is building.
- Several models on the same point. Windy draws on GFS, ECMWF, and the NEMS model from Meteoblue, among others. When they disagree, the day is less certain than a single pretty map suggests.

Save the launches you actually fly and check them the evening before, then again in the morning. Windy does not know your site's rotor, the venturi in the gap, or the local rule about which wind is too much. Use it with a site guide such as Paragliding Map or Spots Guru, and with a look out of the window.

There is a website and an Android app.
