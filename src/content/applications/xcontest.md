XContest is where cross-country flights get scored. You record the flight, upload the IGC file, and the server works out the distance and the points. It is a contest website, not a flight instrument. The instrument is usually XCTrack, which the same team builds, or another logger that writes an IGC file.

Pilots fly it as a world league and as national and club leagues for paragliders and hang gliders. Rankings are split by class, including separate lists for women, EN B, EN C, competition paragliders, and hang glider types. Read the rules of the contest you enter. National rules do not all use the same multipliers.

## How a flight becomes points

An IGC file is a GPS track with a time, a position, and an altitude on each fix. XCTrack writes one for you. Other varios and navigation devices write one too, sometimes only after you download the track with the manufacturer's software.

The scorer places a start, up to three turnpoints, and a finish on your track. A straight line scores as free distance. A closed triangle scores more per kilometer than a straight line, and an FAI triangle, where the shortest leg is at least 28 percent of the whole triangle, scores more again. The exact points per kilometer are in that contest's rules.

You can upload as many flights as you like. A season ranking uses your best flights, often the best six. Keep your own copy of every IGC file. Once it is on the server it is public.

## Uploading

The shortest path is XCTrack: record the flight, then send it to XContest from the track list. You can also upload the file yourself after you log in. Some contests close the claim window a set number of days after the flight, so do not leave a track on the phone for a month.

A track joined together from two devices is rejected. Use one logger for the whole flight.

Live tracks from XContest also feed other apps. Sidekik can show friends who are in the air, and it can import a flight only if that flight was submitted to XContest on the day you flew.
