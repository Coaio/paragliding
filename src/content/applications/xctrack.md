XCTrack turns an Android phone into a flight computer for paraglider pilots. The XContest team builds it, and it is meant for both free flying and competitions. It runs on Android 5.0 or higher.

It is a planning and flying display, not a certified instrument. Keep looking outside, and use whatever logger or vario your airspace and contest rules require.

## On an XC day

- An FAI assistant helps you close a triangle while you are still in the air.
- The track is optimized for the online contest during the flight, so you can see what the flight is worth before you land.
- After landing, one tap uploads the flight to the XContest server.
- Live tracking can share the flight while you are still up.

## In a competition

- Load a task and fly it with competition widgets.
- Share the task by QR code, NFC, email, WhatsApp, or a file.
- Import the usual waypoint formats.

## The instrument

- Thermal assistant and a computed wind.
- Airspace, including temporary activations, plus cable and obstacle warnings, updated from [airspace.xcontest.org](https://airspace.xcontest.org).
- Terrain maps, and road maps in Mapsforge format.
- A display you can rebuild widget by widget, plus glide distance.
- Actions you can bind to hardware keys, including Bluetooth remotes, so you touch the screen less.
- E-ink displays, and head-up displays such as ActiveLook and Everysight Maverick.
- Bluetooth varios and other external devices, plus NMEA over TCP or UDP.
- More than 20 languages, translated by pilots.

The project is under active development. The developers say features are still missing and bugs still turn up. Read the [manual by Bertrand Fontaine](https://www.fly-air3.com/support/xctrack-manual/) before you rely on a new layout in the air, and follow changes on the [public GitLab project](https://gitlab.com/xcontest-public/xctrack-public).
